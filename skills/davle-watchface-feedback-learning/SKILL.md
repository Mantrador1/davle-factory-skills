# DAVLE Watch Face Feedback Learning

## Objective
Turn real DAVLE release outcomes into durable learning while keeping correlation, causation, and speculation separate.

## Accepted evidence
Use available signals such as:
- store impressions,
- product-page views,
- conversion/install rate,
- paid acquisition versus organic traffic when known,
- installs/uninstalls,
- retention or active-device signals when available,
- ratings and review volume,
- review text themes,
- refund or complaint signals when available,
- screenshot or listing A/B results,
- pricing changes,
- update effects,
- support issues.

## Normalize context
Record, when known:
- observation window,
- country/market,
- price,
- promotion state,
- traffic source,
- app version,
- listing assets in use,
- major product changes during the window.

## Learning procedure
1. Separate OBSERVED RESULT from INTERPRETATION.
2. Identify repeated positive and negative signals.
3. Look for plausible confounders before attributing performance to design.
4. Compare against previous DAVLE releases where comparable data exists.
5. Produce hypotheses rather than causal claims when evidence is observational.
6. Assign each learning a confidence level: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW.
7. Propose which market, synthesis, prompt, QA, or portfolio rule should change.
8. Do not silently rewrite reusable skill doctrine from one release; require repeated evidence or a strong failure signal.

## High-value learning classes
- thumbnail/click appeal,
- readability complaints,
- complication usefulness,
- customization demand,
- AOD/battery feedback,
- perceived premium value,
- pricing resistance,
- style/category demand,
- technical defects that distort commercial interpretation.

## Output
Return:
- release evidence summary,
- confirmed observations,
- hypotheses,
- confidence levels,
- reusable learnings,
- proposed skill/reference updates,
- items that require more data.

## Guardrail
Never claim that one design element caused sales performance unless a credible controlled comparison supports that conclusion.
