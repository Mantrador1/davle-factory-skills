# 1960s Watch-Hand Language

## Objective
Create hands that feel plausible for a 1960s tool/chronograph/instrument watch, not modern sci-fi.

## Useful families
- matchstick: narrow rectangular hand, excellent for clean racing chronographs,
- baton: straight, simple and legible,
- syringe: narrow body with a pointed reading tip,
- alpha: tapered body with a defined point,
- dauphine: faceted, elegant and broadening toward the center,
- sword: more forceful tool-watch form,
- skeleton/openworked variants of the above.

## DAVLE vintage dense-dial recommendation
For information-dense faces:
- hour: shorter and slightly broader openworked alpha/baton hybrid,
- minute: visibly longer, narrower openworked alpha/baton hybrid,
- seconds: very thin red needle with small counterweight if desired,
- metal: aged nickel/rhodium/steel or muted warm alloy,
- no white interior when user requests true skeleton,
- small patina only; geometry remains crisp.

## Period effect comes from proportion, not dirt
Use:
- restrained taper,
- thin polished/aged edges,
- crisp tips,
- modest frame width,
- readable contrast.

Avoid:
- exaggerated fantasy tips,
- neon inserts,
- faceted cyberpunk geometry,
- oversized cavities,
- decorative shapes unrelated to reading function.
