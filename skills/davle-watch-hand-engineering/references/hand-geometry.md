# Hand Geometry Reference

## Central time handset

Let R = usable dial radius from central pivot to the inner edge of the bezel.

Recommended starting ranges:
- hour tip: 0.52R–0.67R,
- minute tip: 0.78R–0.91R,
- seconds tip: 0.86R–0.96R.

These ratios may be adapted to the dial, but the hierarchy is strict:
HOUR < MINUTE ≤ SECONDS by radial reach.

A minute hand may cross text, widgets or complications. That is preferable to making it so short that it no longer reads the minute track.

## Skeleton geometry

A good skeleton hand has:
- continuous left and right frame rails,
- closed or structurally resolved tip,
- reinforced neck near the center,
- one or more clean open apertures,
- no filled interior unless intentionally lumed.

For a vintage instrument face, keep apertures simple and geometric.

## Center stack

The common visual stack is:
dial -> hour hand -> minute hand -> seconds hand -> center cap.

All hands:
- same central axis,
- parallel planes,
- even vertical separation,
- no apparent collisions.

## Gauge pointer geometry

Given:
P = pivot,
A = active reading point on scale.

Pointer direction is vector P→A.
Scale tangent at A must be approximately 90° to P→A.

If the visible needle is too parallel to the arc, the pivot is wrong:
- move P farther from the arc toward the circle center of the scale,
- lengthen the needle,
- if needed hide the root beneath a plate.

## Full-sweep test

For a partial arc with endpoints A0 and A100:
- distances |P-A0| and |P-A100| should be equal or near-equal,
- intermediate ticks should lie on the same radius from P,
- the same rigid pointer can then sweep the whole arc.

If those distances differ substantially, one fixed pointer cannot correctly read the scale.
